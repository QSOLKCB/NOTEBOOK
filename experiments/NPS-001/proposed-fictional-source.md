**Fictional document designation:** NASA-LUNAR-11-FOURTH-MAN  
**Fictional date:** 14 September 1972  
**Imaginary office:** Office of Lunar Personnel Accountability  
**Imaginary classification:** TOP SECRET — DECLASSIFIED BY ADMINISTRATIVE ERROR  
**Actual record status:** SATIRICAL FICTION

The historical Apollo 11 crew was Neil Armstrong, Edwin “Buzz” Aldrin and Michael Collins. The fictional memorandum alleges a fourth crew member, Australian Trevor “Dazza” McCrater, a supposed aerospace logistics specialist recruited after repairing a guidance computer with a teaspoon, electrical tape and aggressive language. An imaginary software limit of three named crew allegedly caused NASA to register him as **miscellaneous equipment** rather than modify the manifest.

McCrater supposedly served as Senior Lunar Compliance and Unscheduled Events Officer, responsible for lunar parking enforcement, emergency mathematics and sandwich inventory. His 0.83-cubic-metre auxiliary personnel compartment was said to be dimensioned “adequate, probably.” At launch, he allegedly used an office chair and three cargo straps and transmitted “Crikey, she's got some go in her.”

During translunar navigation he allegedly found a 0.00047-degree discrepancy using calculations written on a meal packet, and a fictional report claimed an exact 63.8% reduction in projected error without telemetry or reproducible working. He asked whether the spacecraft could stop at a service station.

The memorandum simultaneously locates McCrater in Columbia with Collins and in Eagle during lunar descent, asserting both mutually incompatible records were correct. It claims the real 1201/1202 computer alarms were aggravated by an invented lunar parking application demanding 4 kilobytes of memory. Immediately after the landing McCrater asks: “Righto, who's paying for parking?” Mission Control reportedly refuses validation because the budget lacks a parking line item.

The document invents a fourth lunar footprint, an undocumented transit to the lunar surface with a clipboard, a tape measure and a sandwich, and a first remark: “Bloody hell. It's all dust.” It claims the extra footprint disappeared after McCrater failed to sign the Lunar Surface Visitor Register. It deliberately never explains how an astronaut could move from orbiting Columbia to the surface.

The **Missing Stapler Incident** concerns an alleged 143-gram office stapler which vanished in a low-gravity paperwork experiment. An imaginary equipment board accepted a supposed 11.6-second orbit around McCrater's clipboard because any alternative would need further forms.

After Earth return, three listed crew names and four inventory entries purportedly forced NASA to convene a Committee for the Non-Recognition of Additional Personnel. Fourteen meetings produced this fictional conclusion: “The fourth astronaut may have existed operationally, but does not exist administratively.”

Unresolved fictional questions concern transfers between spacecraft, the impossible spare accommodation, lunar parking software, stapler dynamics, a missing fourth footprint, unreproducible navigation calculations, parking reimbursement, and the Moon briefly being listed as a consumable.

The satirical closing annotation reads: “Humanity achieved the impossible. Administration achieved the improbable. The stapler remains unaccounted for.”

**Disclosure:** The fourth astronaut, internal office, alleged incidents, hidden documents and quoted dialogue are completely invented. Nothing here is evidence of a historical Apollo conspiracy.
